Why it rolls up

Give every tick the same square tile and put each layer one tile further out. Ring m is then only m tiles long at distance m from the tip, but a flat circle that far out needs 2π·m. The skin can't lie flat, so it rolls up into a horn about 9° off true. The wheel on the main page is this same skin with every ring stretched 2π times so it lies flat.

Down the back runs the seam, and the lines of ticks beside it run straight down the horn. Around the front they tilt and meet in a herringbone on the half-turn. On the first half of the turn a tick lines up with the same tick on the next layer; on the second half, with the next tick along.

Tick width sets the body. Wider tiles open the horn into a cone. At exactly 2π it is the flat page, and no whole-number ratio ever lands there. Wider still, there is more ring than a flat disk can hold, so the skin ruffles like kale.
