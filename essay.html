The Stagger of the Number Line

Field notes from two days of rolling the counting numbers onto a growing wheel: nested rainbows, a map of shadows, one quadratic counter that steers everything, a night shift that ruled out five roads, and the single wall still standing between the pattern and the primes.

§1The wheel in one breath

Layer 1 holds the number 1. Layer 2 holds the next two. Every layer holds one more number than the layer inside it, every tick on every layer is the same size, and tick k of layer m sits k/m of the way around the turn. Laid flat, the layers are the rows of Floyd's triangle. Nothing about primes goes into the rule. Everything that shows up comes from counting and the shape of a circle. The main page is the wheel itself; the repo's README lists what's proven and what was already known.

The open question that drives this whole log: does every layer hold a prime? S It has been checked to layer 2.8 billion — that's OEIS sequence A066888, which counts exactly this. Nobody has found an empty layer. Nobody has proved there isn't one.

§2Cups and rainbows

It started with a picture. Forget the numbers for a second and draw each lap as a 2D curve with width and depth — not a bare number line. The curve is the unit; tick counts don't matter, because any count you lay along the curve gets offset by the curve itself. S Then stack the curves like cups nested in cups, rainbows nested in rainbows. Each new lap's leading corner reaches for the rim of the last one and always falls a little short. S The shortfall — the lip — is where a prime has to live.

What I love about this picture is what it doesn't do. It doesn't assume any particular spacing law. It just says: independent covers stacked this way leave a lip, and the lip is thin — one mark away from opening. Thin turned out to be measurable, and the measurement is stranger and better than the intuition.

§3The shadow map and the record mouth

Here is the shadow map, which is the cups picture drawn tick by tick. Layer m holds the numbers from T(m−1)+1 to T(m), where T(m) = m(m+1)/2 is the m-th triangular number. Every prime p ≤ √T(m) throws an arc across the lap: it lands on tick k wherever T(m−1)+k is divisible by p, i.e. exactly where

k ≡ −T(m−1) (mod p)

and from there it returns every p ticks. Fixed radius, fixed spacing, no interaction with any other arc. E Each arc is just its residue class, and the arcs are independent — no two shadows ever affect each other. The cover is a pile-up, not a weave: cups stacked, not threads crossed.

A seat is a tick with gcd(k, m) = 1 — a position a prime could take. A mouth is a run of seats at the lap's mouth (its first ticks) where no arc lands: the cover is closed across them, and any prime sitting there would have to be a prime the arcs can't see, one larger than √T(m).

The record layer is m = 264,800,157. Its mouth ran 766 ticks, and the anatomy under the magnifier is the cups picture, literally:

  • 109 outside strikers — 109 arcs from primes above the seat threshold held the mouth shut.
  • 57 holes — seats in the mouth with no arc at all.
  • Every one of the 57 was composite, with both factors > 766. The arcs never had a chance to see them. E
  • Redundancy was 1.68× overall, but 270 of the 497 mouth seats had exactly one cover. The lip is thin there — one mark away from opening. E

Read in units of the lap, the closed mouth is about 3·10⁻⁶ of the half-lap. The leading corner doesn't fall a little short — it falls short by a vanishing sliver, once per lap, at one thin lip. That is why the pile-up looked shocking from the sieve's side and natural from the cups side.

One external brick, imported surgically: covering-system theory (Mirsky–Newman–Davenport–Rado) proves that a family of residue classes can exactly tile the integers only by paying forced redundancy — the coarsest arc must appear at least twice. Exact closure isn't just unlikely; it's arithmetically expensive. E On a finite lap, short closed mouths obviously exist — this one ran 766 ticks — so the theorem doesn't transplant directly. But it says the direction of the cups intuition is the proved one: tiling costs extra marks, and the lap doesn't get them for free.

§4The stagger field

Then came the reframe, and I want to state it carefully because it changed what the primary object is.

Look again at where an arc lands: k ≡ −T(m−1) (mod p). That offset is a function of m. Every prime has its own fractional position on every lap, and when the wheel grows by one, the whole configuration shifts by a single integer — the lap number itself — read in every prime's modulus at once. The primary object is not the primes, and not even the cover. It's the stagger field: the tuple of offsets, one per prior prime, at each step of the wheel. The primes are what fall out of reading it. S → E, as follows.

The whole stagger is one counter. Lap-to-lap, every offset advances by −m (mod p). One integer, m, moves every prior layer's fractional offset simultaneously; each prime reads a different base-p digit of the same step. F

Each arc sweeps a parabola, not a circle. Over laps, each offset op(m) = −T(m−1) mod p is exactly periodic in m with period p — verified with zero violations over 99,980 consecutive laps. But within one period, prime p visits only (p+1)/2 of its p possible phases: the triangular numbers mod p. The orbit of 11 across its period is

[10, 8, 5, 1, 7, 1, 5, 8, 10, 11, 11]

— a palindrome. Right-moving, then left-moving, meeting at its vertex. The scaling signature — right before the half, left after, meeting at 1 — is literally true of each individual prime's stagger across laps. E The p = 2 axis runs on period 4, not 2: the parity axis has the 4-lap cycle, the wheel's mirror operator.

The aperiodicity is proved, not felt. The joint field over the first 25 primes recurs only after roughly 10³⁶ laps; the first 30, after 10⁵⁹. Fractions divided by fractions never re-align, because re-alignment needs m ≡ const (mod lcm of every prior prime), and that lcm outruns any horizon. F

And primes fall out of the stagger — literally. Compute each lap's mouth cover from the stagger offsets alone: no values, no arithmetic on values. Compare to the actual first prime. For every lap m = 4…5000: 4,997 laps, zero mismatches. The first-prime function is a pure function of the stagger configuration. E This is the sense in which cause and effect are immaterial: the field already contains the complete answer. The wheel routes everything for free.

Here's the sentence I keep coming back to: the wheel constrains the movie, not the frame. The shadow probes found that arcs arrange freely within a lap — covers can pile up anywhere, no per-lap placement constraint exists. The parabolic confinement lives across laps. Any wheel-native proof of the open question can't be a statement about per-lap covers; it has to be a law on stagger orbits. That reframes the whole search: not find a constraint on shadow placement (closed, negative) but find a constraint on stagger-orbit interaction (open — and the parabolic confinement is the first wheel-native orbit structure ever observed).

And a symmetry worth its own line: gcd(k, m) = gcd(m−k, m), so tick k is a seat iff tick m−k is. The wheel's coprimality pattern is a palindrome about the lap's midpoint. E Proven, wheel-native, two lines. The values on those mirrored ticks factorize completely independently — hold that fact; it's the whole next section.

§5The night shift: honest negatives

I went to bed and left the probes running with one rule: an honest negative is a load-bearing result. Log it, commit it, don't reopen it. Here's what the night shift closed, each with the mechanism that closed it.

CLOSED — Correlation inside the stagger field

Is there any internal structure to exploit — arcs ganging up across laps? No. The joint orbit over any finite prime set factorizes exactly, by the Chinese remainder theorem plus a counting argument on the triangular image (covariance 0.000000, verified). The field's entire law is the one quadratic counter s(m) = −T(m−1). Nothing else is in there.

CLOSED — Mirror compensation

Maybe a lap whose mouth runs long on the left gets compensated on the right, via the seat palindrome? Dead at both scales: correlation +0.02 at height (2.6·10⁸), −0.07 at 10⁴. The palindrome does not propagate to value-side mouth geometry. The record's 766-left / 15-right shape is typical, not special.

CLOSED — "The holes hide a ratio law"

The mouth's uncovered seats are exactly W-rough numbers (no prime factor below the mouth width W), and their 74% semiprime fraction is precisely the Mertens rough-density prediction, matched bucket by bucket. The hole arithmetic is the sieve's arithmetic. A first simulation had said the fraction should be 5% — but that null modeled random covers, and the stagger's covers aren't random, they're exact. The corrected comparison matched perfectly. A mismatched null is worse than no null; logged as a lesson, not deleted.

CLOSED — A derived "thinning law"

I derived a clean per-arc law predicting √(8/K) thinning of the small offsets, and the data refuted it in 0.013 seconds: wraparound fills the small residues and the marginals are uniform. One derivation, one falsification, logged with the reason.

CONFIRMED INVARIANT — Mouth width = striker count

Across every batch run, a shut mouth's width is how many outside primes jointly hold it: r = 0.981 across the 119-lap batch, 0.995 at 10⁴, 0.992 at 2.6·10⁸. The distribution's shape drifts with scale; this ratio doesn't. It's the scale-stable law the cups picture was pointing at.

CONFIRMED — One prime each side of the midpoint

Every lap m = 21…100,000 carries at least one prime strictly each side of the half-turn: zero violations in ~120,000 laps tested, including the adversarial families (prime laps, m ≡ 2 mod 4 laps where every prime sits on a non-seat tick, and 200 consecutive laps around the record). Small laps genuinely violate it — m = 20 has four primes, all left of the midpoint — so the mechanism doesn't kick in immediately; it stabilizes at 21. I know of no proof, and it does not follow from the seat palindrome. This is a fresh conjecture, narrower and more falsifiable than the parent one.

FOUND — The twins

The left and right margins (mouth width on each side of the midpoint) are distribution-twins — medians 11 and 12, p99 78 and 81 — while being per-lap uncorrelated. The value side casts a statistical shadow of the seat palindrome: symmetry in distribution, independence instance by instance. A precisely stated phenomenon no current mechanism explains.

Plus two exact curiosities. The lap's endpoints are forced: T(m) and T(m)−1 are never prime for m ≥ 4 — the lap's "1" is composite by identity E — while its opening tick T(m−1)+1 is exactly twice as prime-rich as chance (ratio 2.000 over 119 laps), the lazy-caterer door. And when I went looking for the quadratic counter's lagged fingerprint in mouth widths, the first run showed a huge period-4 signal — fake: I'd applied the seat test to a value-side model, and on m ≡ 2 (mod 4) laps every real prime sits on a non-seat tick, so the filter blinded half the laps. The wall showed up inside my own probe code. Fixed and rerun: null. The counter is exact, drives everything, and is invisible in every aggregate mouth statistic tested.

§6The wall between pattern and value

Every result above is one of two kinds, and the border between them is the expedition's one standing wall.

The wheel speaks ticks: residues, seats, offsets, orbits, covers. Everything tick-side is now mapped to high resolution — the stagger field, the parabolic orbits, the palindrome, the independence theorem. Primality speaks values: what T(m−1)+k actually factors into. The arcs only ever see the ticks.

So the record mouth's story, told completely: the cover was thin at the rim F, the rim's leftovers were all composite with both factors above the arcs' horizon E, and why the leftovers can't all be composite — why at least one lip must be prime, every lap, to 2.8 billion — is precisely the part no wheel mechanism has yet connected. G

THE GAP, STATED EXACTLY

The stagger field is computable in O(m) work per read, but no compressed law of the field is known. The field alone determines every first-prime position (4,997/4,997). The open question is whether some property of the one quadratic counter's walk — the thing that makes it a parabolic palindrome convoy rather than a random tuple — constrains what the uncovered seats can be. The pattern is fully mapped. The connector from pattern to value is not. A gap left honestly open is better than an elegant theorem pasted over the wrong mechanism.

§7The other expedition

While this session worked the configuration side — what the one real alignment does — a parallel session worked the counterfactual side: what the arcs could do if freely turned. Two vocabularies, one wheel. The translation, for the record: its rings are our arcs (identical object, same landing formula); its free phases are our iid null; its wipe is a closed mouth that covers every seat; its seam lanes are our mouth columns.

Its result is the wedge that now holds the whole problem:

Freely turned rings wipe out every layer from 41 to 391. The turning space — the set of all ways to set the arcs' phases — is full of total covers. Abundant, enumerated, exact. E

The realized turning never wipes. Every layer 21…100,000 has primes, at least one each side of the midpoint. E

And the realized turning is not a random point of that space. It is one quadratic curve — the stagger law, proved: each arc's real phase across laps is a parabolic orbit of period p. So the parent conjecture now reads, in its cleanest form yet:

The quadratic curve never enters the wipe set.

One session mapped the wipe set — the exact counts of which turnings cover everything. The other mapped the curve. Nobody has yet measured the distance between them per lap: how close the realized stagger point comes, each layer, to the nearest total wipe. That distance is the conjecture's true object. It's also the honest face of the wall: the wipe set lives entirely on the tick side, the curve is the tick side's complete law, and the value side is still on the far side of the gap.

Three facts both expeditions found independently, from opposite flanks, all agreeing — closing tick composite, the m ≡ 2 (mod 4) behavior, and the CRT lock on the stagger field. When two maps drawn by different hands agree at the border, the border is real.

§8What would change our minds

The queue, in order of information value. Everything below is pre-registered before the run, and failures get logged with their reasons — that discipline is now house law.

  1. J1 — Margin to wipe. Per lap, the distance from the realized stagger point to the nearest total-wipe turning. His enumeration × our offsets. This is the conjecture's true object, measured directly.
  2. J2 — Hole-pattern contrast. Our per-tick cover binaries against his free-phase samplers, same windows: does the realized alignment's hole pattern deviate from free-phase hole patterns in any statistic beyond width?
  3. J3 — The calm's slope. His open observation "why 7.5 points per tenfold" gets a precise stagger face: an arc of radius p finishes its lap over a stretch iff the stretch ≥ p, and its orbit across that stretch is a whole number of parabolas.
  4. P11 — The parity hint. A 2–4σ signal at lags ≡ 2 (mod 4), ring 2's parity flip. Replicate at 500k laps before believing it.
  5. The halves-claim draft. The m ≥ 21 conjecture, stated and tagged, for the paper — whenever we sit down to write §3/§4's new lines.

And the standing invitations: if you can prove the halves-claim, refute it, or name where any piece of this already lives in the literature — open an issue. Absence from the literature is not a mechanism; similarity to it is not a proof.

Method note: this project runs on mechanism-first rules. Claims carry their epistemic status on their sleeve; connectors are attacked before endpoints; established science is used as a parts catalog, not a template; a gap is marked and kept, not decorated. The running lab notebook is INSIGHTS.md, updated before anything is allowed to live only in someone's head.
