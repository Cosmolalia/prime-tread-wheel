The Donut View

A 4D creature turns two planes at once, so it counts on two clocks at once. The numbers wind a knot around a donut, and every number sits on both clocks.

Reading the donut
An open cell: neither clock shares a factor with the numbers there. Every prime past the clocks' own factors lands in one. Closed by the first clock (the long way around). Closed by the second clock (the short way around the tube). Closed by both. The count: one step moves both clocks one notch, so it winds around like a knot. Where the count is now.
Clocks

4D tread wheel

Both clocks grow by one each layer: layer m is one full lap of clocks m and m+1, so it holds m × (m+1) numbers.

Could the rings wipe out a layer?

For each layer of each wheel: the most numbers in a row its deciding rings could cover, turned any way (ring 2 kept on the evens), divided by the layer's width. Above 1, some turning wipes the layer out. Across: the size of the numbers, on a log scale.
