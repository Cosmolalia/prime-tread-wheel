## Dimensions (new, to test now)
- Spheres can't hold counting: m points spread perfectly evenly exist on a circle for every m, on the ordinary sphere only for 4, 6, 8, 12, 20.
- Every two dimensions add one independent rotation plane = one more clock. 2D/3D: one clock (our wheel). 4D/5D: two clocks, points on a torus, counting path = torus knot. Infinite D: one plane per ring = the Platonic Map.
- CRT on the donut: the open spots of a single ring of size ab look scattered; on the a x b torus they are a perfect product grid (open rows x open columns). The scatter is the shadow of flattening a grid onto one circle. Primes = where one straight line (the count) threads a growing grid.
- 4D tread wheel guess: both planes grow by one each layer, periods (m, m+1): layer m holds m(m+1) numbers, totals m(m+1)(m+2)/3 (pyramid numbers, twice tetrahedral). Layer width ~ x^(2/3) vs 2D's x^(1/2).
- Ladder of layer widths: 2D x^(1/2), 4D x^(2/3), 6D x^(3/4), ... k clocks x^(k/(k+1)). Known: primes in [x, x + x^0.525] for large x (unconditional); even RH only gives sqrt(x) log x. So 2D sits right AT the square-root barrier; 4D and up are past it (primes between consecutive cubes-type results).
- Prediction: in 4D, free phases can never wipe a layer (free capacity ~ sqrt(x) polylog << x^(2/3)): every 4D layer safe by width alone, like 2D layers 2-17 and like Goldbach. 2D is the only rung where the rings can aim at a whole layer.

## Followed after the donut (Oct 8, afternoon)
- Safe by width is the STRONG form of "every layer holds a prime": if no turning of the deciding rings covers the layer, then the real turning doesn't either, so some number on the layer has no ring under its square root dividing it, so it is prime. Alignment stops mattering.
- 2D: the layer is only sqrt2 times the biggest deciding ring. The most the rings can cover in a row grows faster than the biggest ring (proven lower bound ~ y log y logloglog y / loglog y), so freely turned rings overpower 2D layers (from 41 in our counts). 2D primes depend on the real alignment. That is the knife edge.
- 4D: the layer is about 2 x y^(4/3), y = biggest deciding ring. "Safe forever" needs the rings' longest run Y(y) to grow no faster than y^(4/3). Best PROVEN ceiling: Iwaniec 1978, Y(y) << y^2 (about the whole count). Even whether Y(y) = o(y^2) is open (Erdos problem 687). Conjectured (Maier-Pomerance): Y(y) << y (log y)^(2+o(1)), which would make every rung above 2D safe by a mile. So P6 past layer 60 sits on an open problem; every count says it holds.
- Same quantity both ways: our "most the rings can cover in a row" (h(k)-1) is exactly Erdos's Y(x). The wheel turns his question into: how wide does a layer have to be before the rings can't aim at all of it? 2D: too narrow (proven). 4D and up: wide enough (conjectured, counted to layer 60 / 22).
- Ladder of k clocks: layer width y^(2k/(k+1)), creeping up to y^2 as k grows. The proven ceiling y^2 only meets the ladder at its top.
- Flag ladder: 2D ring + mirror 64%, 4D 72%, 6D 80%; with a clock for every period, every composite is flagged and only primes stand = the Platonic Map. The map is what an infinite-D counter sees.
- Atlas P6 check cell now says this (rev 21), with the Erdos 687 link.

## P9: is the calm real? (PRE-REGISTERED 13:55 UTC Oct 8, written just before the run started)
Claim: seam lanes are calmer than coin flips, the calm grows steadily with the length of the stretch counted, and the rings' fixed schedule makes it.
Mechanism behind it: ring p's schedule repeats every p layers. Over a stretch of W layers, rings much smaller than W finish many laps, so their hits come out exact, no luck. Only rings bigger than the stretch add luck. Each tenfold longer stretch lets about one more decade of rings finish their laps, and each decade takes about the same slice of luck, so the calm should grow as a straight line in log(stretch).
Test set: 300 fresh lanes, c = 14..313 (lane 13 excluded: used for a timing run and its first rungs were seen; lane 1 short-range numbers were also seen during validation, and they sat where the plan below already expected). Layers 100,001 to 100,100,000 (1e8 layers each), every value settled exactly. Ratio R = sum (count - expected)^2 / sum coin-flip variance, pooled over lanes and windows, at windows of 1e2 .. 1e8 layers. Coin-flip model: each odd value prime with the gear-rule chance, independently.
(a) Real lanes, whole run: R < 0.6.
(b) Real lanes: R falls at every x10 step from 100 to 10 million layers, and R(100) >= 0.8.
(c) Steady fall: a straight line against log(window) through the six rungs 1e2..1e7 misses no rung by more than 0.05, and the drop per x10 is between 0.04 and 0.10.
(d) The schedule makes the calm: pure coin flips give R between 0.8 and 1.2 at every rung; rings up to 1,000 on their real schedule plus coin flips give R(whole run) < 0.85.
P9 RESULT (14:19 UTC, 300 lanes c = 14..313, layers 100,001..100,100,000, ~3e10 values, exact): ALL FOUR HELD.
- Real lanes, ratio to coin flips by stretch: 100 layers 0.890 | 1e3 0.814 | 1e4 0.741 | 1e5 0.671 | 1e6 0.597 | 1e7 0.509 | whole run (1e8) 0.446.
- Straight line through 1e2..1e7: drop 0.075 per tenfold, worst miss 0.0066 (!). The line predicts 0.441 at 1e8; measured 0.446.
- Pure coin flips: 1.000 1.000 1.000 0.995 0.973 0.972 0.918 (fine; last rung has only 300 windows).
- Rings up to 1,000 on schedule + coin flips: 0.891 0.820 0.765 0.729 0.704 0.689 0.636. FINGERPRINT: tracks the real lanes for short stretches, then FLATTENS near 0.7 once the stretch passes ~10,000 layers, because there are no more scheduled rings left to finish their laps. Real lanes keep calming because every ring is on schedule. The calm is made by rings finishing laps.
- Per lane, whole run: 86% of lanes land within one coin-flip spread of the gear rule (coin flips: 68%); rms 0.68.
- Exploratory, not pre-registered: richer half 0.427 vs poorer half 0.487 at the whole run (within noise); short stretches nearly identical.
- Known for plain primes: Montgomery-Soundararajan (Primes in short intervals, Comm. Math. Phys. 2004, arXiv math/0409258): primes in intervals of length H vary like H log(N/H), not H log N as Cramer's coin-flip model says -> calmer, falling as a straight line in log H. The lanes follow the same straight-line law; the wheel shows the gears (rings finishing laps), and the schedule cut-off experiment isolates it. The law is theirs, conditional on Hardy-Littlewood; the lane-by-lane check and the cut-off picture are ours.
